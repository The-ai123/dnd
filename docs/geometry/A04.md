## Implementation

- Obstacles are convex polygons or finite wall segments; decompose concave shapes explicitly. A square/rectangular axis-aligned token footprint inflates each obstacle by its Minkowski sum. The center-domain bounds shrink by half the footprint. Overlapping inflated geometry contributes union-boundary intersections to the graph. Both clockwise and counterclockwise input vertices normalize to a convex hull; concave vertex sets are rejected.
- Geometric routing uses A* over free obstacle, union and map boundary vertices. Straight-line distance is admissible for both objectives because each edge costs at least its physical length. The route uses continuous positions. Touching is allowed within the approved 1e-6-ft numeric tolerance. Creature collision remains a rules and integration decision.
- Difficult terrain in this spike is rectangular and ordinary ×2 only. Segment/rectangle clipping computes exact entry/exit parameters; the union of intervals prevents overlap compounding. Cost follows the center path. Boundary-aligned travel is charged as outside the terrain, representing the limit of a route just outside; this prototype convention and center-path terrain classification require downstream geometry/editor review, and do not approve arbitrary custom terrain costs.
- Weighted search splits each terrain edge at intersections with other terrain, inflated barriers and the map boundary before placing samples. It refines feasible candidates continuously along terrain edges. It certifies a candidate when its cost minus the strongest lower bound is at most max(0.1 ft, 0.5% of that lower bound).
- Visibility includes exact wall/radius point queries and endpoint-ray visibility polygons with <=0.05-ft circle chord error. Doors open both movement and sight; the flags remain independent. Polygon interior tangency follows the numeric tolerance; line-wall contact blocks. No elevation, lighting, special senses, player projection or fog persistence is implied.
- Explored cells are a sparse set allocated on explicit reveal. Actual visible-area-to-cell rasterization, historical fog and reconstruction belong to E07. The 5-ft exploration-cell demonstration does not quantize token geometry or establish the final fog resolution. `isCurrent` checks world/scene/revision/request identity; actual worker dispatch/cancellation and authoritative commit checks remain integration work.

## Lower bounds

The geometric visibility graph supplies a distance bound on nominal geometry. Weighted paths cost at least their physical length. The kernel combines that bound with three stronger ones.

1. **Sampling error.** In the nominal arrangement, a free ordinary-cost portal is a straight terrain boundary fragment. An optimum can replace everything between its first and last contact with that portal by the straight boundary run. The run costs at most the subpath it replaces. Splitting at all intersections prevents the replacement from introducing contacts with other portal interiors. A fragment inside difficult terrain has constant cost on both sides and needs no refraction vertex. The remaining homogeneous legs can use polygonal visibility vertices.
2. For a portal with sample spacing `h`, its at most two contact points each move by at most `h/2`. A moved point affects at most two legs, each of weight at most two. Within a homogeneous region, a leg can reach the moved point along the portal and then use a shortest visibility route in the closure. This gives a total additive sampling error at most `4 × sum(h)`. Subtracting that error from the sampled graph optimum yields a conservative continuous lower bound. The error shrinks with spacing. The dense graph and current sample cap make that bound too loose for most workload queries.
3. **Terrain strips.** A cheaper path lies in the ellipse whose foci are its endpoints and whose major diameter is the candidate cost. A difficult strip covering a whole cross section of that ellipse is unavoidable. A scalar potential with transverse slope `p` and longitudinal slope `sqrt(w² − p²)` has gradient norm `w` in each layer. Its endpoint difference bounds the cost of any eligible route. The proof preserves ordinary-cost seams by requiring strict coverage across the strip.
4. **Isolated rectangles.** A run entering and exiting the same or adjacent faces can be replaced by ordinary-cost boundary travel. An opposite-face transit pays at least its crossing width in addition to geometric length. The search branches on avoiding or crossing selected isolated rectangles. Its lower bound is the smallest bound among surviving branches. The shortcut requires a free rectangle perimeter and no other difficult region touching it. Rectangles containing either endpoint skip this check.

A follow-up interval-portal lower-bound design was rejected during investigation: charging minimum distances between boundary intervals permits a chain of adjacent intervals to walk a whole boundary at zero cost. Finer subdivision does not resolve that degeneracy. Such a graph can be conservative yet fail to converge; it is not a valid completion strategy and is not included as a purported optimizer.
